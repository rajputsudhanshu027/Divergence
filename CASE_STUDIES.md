# Country case-study notes

These are concise, reproducible examples of observed development change. They are prompts for comparative analysis, not causal evaluations. The numeric endpoint values below are from the World Bank Indicators API snapshot retrieved 22 November 2026. Revisions can change these values.

## Bangladesh — expanding electricity access

World Bank access-to-electricity observations rise from **32.0% in 2000 to 99.4% in 2022** (+67.4 percentage points). The World Bank's account of the Bangladesh solar-home-systems program says rural electricity access was 27% in early 2003 and describes the 2003 Rural Electrification and Renewable Energy Development project as a two-track effort: solar home systems in rural areas alongside support for the grid and rural cooperatives. These sources document temporal coincidence and program context; they do not establish how much of the national trend was caused by any one program. The rural 2003 estimate and national indicator series have different populations and should not be compared as if they were the same measure.

**Explorer recipe:** Select Bangladesh; choose *Access to electricity (% of population)*; set the period to 2000–2022 and baseline to 2000. Vary the matching features and scaling. The line is a national annual series; compare it with peers only after checking the coverage table and peer identities.

Sources: [World Bank API series, Bangladesh, EG.ELC.ACCS.ZS, 2000–2022](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/BGD/indicator/EG.ELC.ACCS.ZS?date=2000%3A2022&format=json&per_page=100); [World Bank, “A Game-changer in Bangladesh’s Growth Story: Solar Home Systems”](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/feature/2021/04/08/a-game-changer-in-bangladesh-growth-story-solar-home-systems); [World Bank, *Living in the Light: The Bangladesh Solar Home Systems Story*](https://documents.worldbank.org/en/publication/documents-reports/documentdetail/153291616567928411/living-in-the-light-the-bangladesh-solar-home-systems-story); [Bangladesh Government Aid Information Management System, IDCOL Solar Home Systems Project](https://aims.erd.gov.bd/Project/View/1891) (independent official project registry and program objective).

## Ethiopia — electricity access remains incomplete

The national access-to-electricity series rises from **12.7% in 2000 to 55.4% in 2023** (+42.7 percentage points), while leaving a large share of the population without reported access. The World Bank describes Ethiopia's 2017 National Electrification Program as an integrated approach combining grid and off-grid options. A separate World Bank feature describes last-mile mini-grid work under the Ethiopia Electrification Program. This is useful context for how the government and partners approached access constraints, not evidence that those programs alone produced the national trend.

**Explorer recipe:** Select Ethiopia; choose *Access to electricity (% of population)*; set the period to 2000–2023 and baseline to 2000. Inspect both endpoint coverage and peers; the national percentage does not show reliability, affordability, or the distribution of access within the country.

Sources: [World Bank API series, Ethiopia, EG.ELC.ACCS.ZS, 2000–2023](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/ETH/indicator/EG.ELC.ACCS.ZS?date=2000%3A2023&format=json&per_page=100); [World Bank, “Lighting Up Eastern Africa: How Greater Access to Energy is Creating Jobs and Improving Public Services in Rural Ethiopia”](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/feature/2025/01/24/lighting-up-eastern-africa-access-to-energy-afe-rural-ethiopia); [World Bank, Ethiopia Electrification Program Implementation Status and Results Report](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099100625090051336/pdf/P160395-fbc8fd91-7e29-4313-a2c9-4efd0934d33b.pdf); [Ethiopia Ministry of Water and Energy, *Ethiopian Energy Sector Brief 2026*](https://mowe.gov.et/sites/default/files/resource/Ethiopian%20Energy%20Sector%20Brief%202026.pdf) (government description of the National Electrification Program's grid and off-grid tracks and access targets).

## Viet Nam — long-run growth with a recent poverty reversal

World Bank GDP-per-capita data (constant 2015 US$) rise from **$595.49 in 1984 to $3,617.66 in 2022**, about 6.1 times the 1984 level. The World Bank's 2024 Poverty and Equity Brief describes early Đổi Mới reforms, demographic characteristics, and expanding consumer markets as complementary ingredients in long-run growth; that contextual account does not identify a single cause. It also reports an increase in the lower-middle-income poverty rate from 3.8% in 2020 to 4.2% in 2022 under its $3.65/day (2017 PPP) line. That poverty line differs from this app's older $2.15/day outcome series, so users must not treat those poverty measures as interchangeable.

**Explorer recipe:** Select Vietnam; choose *GDP per capita (constant 2015 US$)*; set the period to 1984–2022 and baseline to 1984. The app's outcome slider starts at 1980 to support this comparison. Check whether the peer set changes under robust normalization and outcome-only matching. For poverty context, read the brief directly rather than substituting its newer poverty line into the app's `SI.POV.DDAY` indicator.

Sources: [World Bank API series, Viet Nam, NY.GDP.PCAP.KD, 1984–2022](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/VNM/indicator/NY.GDP.PCAP.KD?date=1984%3A2022&format=json&per_page=100); [World Bank, Viet Nam Poverty & Equity Brief, October 2024](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099454201032528439/pdf/IDU188a7a5aa1c7bd14688184e2195b9aba24b8b.pdf); [World Bank, 2022 Viet Nam Poverty and Equity Assessment](https://www.worldbank.org/en/country/vietnam/publication/2022-vietnam-poverty-and-equity-assessment-report); [Viet Nam General Statistics Office, Socio-economic situation in the fourth quarter and 2022](https://www.gso.gov.vn/en/data-and-statistics/2023/01/socio-economic-situation-in-the-fourth-quarter-and-2022/) (independent national-accounts context: the office reported 8.02% overall real GDP growth for 2022, a different measure from the cited per-capita level series).

## Shared limitations

The API's national indicators are not always annual survey measurements: sources, estimation methods, and reference years can differ across measures. The app's fixed peer universe is not a representative sample, and peer matching cannot adjust for all relevant structures or shocks. Government and statistical-office sources add independent institutional context, but do not independently reproduce every World Bank series value; program material does not establish causal attribution. Re-check the API before reuse because the World Bank revises series and poverty-line conventions evolve.
